# Northbank Logistics: Supply Chain Analytics Dataset

> **This dataset is entirely fictional.** Northbank Logistics, its suppliers, customers, products and every number in these files were invented and generated by `generate_data.py`. Any resemblance to real companies or people is coincidental. Nothing here is real business data.

A synthetic but internally consistent dataset for practising supply chain analytics: data cleaning, order fulfilment (OTIF), backlog analysis, supplier performance, stock-outs and labour productivity.

## Regenerate the data

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt   # pandas + numpy only
python generate_data.py
```

The script uses a fixed random seed (`42`), so every run produces byte-identical files in `data/`.

## The scenario

Northbank Logistics is a (fictional) UK warehouse business that stores and ships trade orders for 300 products across six categories. It runs seven days a week with a **Day** and a **Night** shift and buys stock from 12 suppliers in the UK, Europe and Asia.

The data covers **90 days: 18 August 2026 to 15 November 2026**.

The warehouse was simulated day by day (orders arrive, staff pick and dispatch the oldest orders first, stock goes down, purchase orders arrive and stock goes back up), and all seven tables were built from that one simulation. They agree with each other:

- every order line belongs to an order, and `lines_ordered` / `lines_shipped` match `order_lines.csv`
- for every SKU and day: `on_hand(today) = on_hand(yesterday) + received − shipped`
- units and orders picked in `labour_daily.csv` equal what was actually dispatched that day

## Files

| File | Rows | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `orders.csv` | ~6,160 | One row per customer order |
| `order_lines.csv` | ~20,500 | One row per SKU on an order |
| `products.csv` | 300 | Product master |
| `inventory_daily.csv` | 27,000 | End-of-day stock, one row per SKU per day (300 × 90) |
| `suppliers.csv` | 12 | Supplier master |
| `purchase_orders.csv` | ~630 | Replenishment orders to suppliers |
| `labour_daily.csv` | 180 | Staffing and output, one row per shift per day (90 × 2) |

### `orders.csv`
| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| `order_id` | `ORD-000001` and up |
| `order_date` | Date the customer placed the order |
| `customer_id` | `CUST-0001` to `CUST-0800`. Each customer is always in one region. |
| `region` | UK delivery region |
| `promised_date` | Date the customer was promised delivery |
| `dispatched_date` | Date the order left the warehouse |
| `delivered_date` | Date the order reached the customer |
| `lines_ordered` | Number of lines on the order |
| `lines_shipped` | Number of lines shipped **in full** (`qty_shipped = qty_ordered`) |
| `status` | `Delivered`, `In Transit` (dispatched, delivery after 15 Nov), `Awaiting Dispatch`, or `Cancelled` |

### `order_lines.csv`
`order_id`, `sku`, `qty_ordered`, `qty_shipped`. If a SKU is short, the line ships what is available and the shortfall is dropped (no back-orders). An order is only held back if none of its lines can be picked.

### `products.csv`
`sku` (`NB-10001`…), `description`, `category`, `unit_cost`, `unit_price` (GBP), `supplier_id`, `reorder_point` (units), `lead_time_days` (the supplier's quoted lead time).

### `inventory_daily.csv`
`date`, `sku`, `on_hand` (units in the warehouse at end of day), `allocated` (units reserved for open orders that have not yet been dispatched, never more than `on_hand`).

### `suppliers.csv`
`supplier_id`, `name`, `country`.

### `purchase_orders.csv`
`po_id`, `supplier_id`, `sku`, `qty_ordered`, `qty_received`, `order_date`, `promised_date`, `received_date`. POs still outstanding on 15 November have a blank `received_date` and `qty_received = 0`. That is expected.

### `labour_daily.csv`
`date`, `shift` (`Day`/`Night`), `operatives`, `hours_worked` (total hours across all operatives on that shift), `units_picked` (units picked and packed), `orders_picked`.

## Things to know before you start

- Dates are ISO format (`YYYY-MM-DD`). Blank means no value.
- Some blanks are legitimate: orders not yet dispatched or delivered, and POs not yet received at the end of the period.
- The files are exports from operational systems, so treat them the way you would treat real data. Check them before you trust them.

## Suggested questions

1. What is the on-time and in-full (OTIF) rate overall, by region and by week?
2. Was there a period when the warehouse fell behind? How big did the backlog get, and how long did it take to recover?
3. Which suppliers deliver on time and in full? How does lateness affect stock?
4. Which products ran out of stock, and what do they have in common?
5. How productive is each shift (units per hour)? Does it change over time?
6. How much sales value was lost to short-shipped lines?

### Data quality problems in `orders.csv`

| Problem | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Duplicate orders** | 121 rows are exact copies of another row (about 2%). 6,163 rows but only 6,042 unique `order_id`s. `order_lines.csv` is **not** duplicated, so the duplicates inflate order counts. |
| **Inconsistent region spellings** | 363 orders (about 6%) use a non-standard spelling, giving 46 distinct values for 9 real regions. Examples: `london`, `LONDON`, `Londn`, `S.East`, `SE`, `N. West`, `North-West`, `Mid lands`, `Yorks`, `Scotland ` (trailing space), `Cymru`. |
| **Blank dates** | 35 planted blanks: 15 `dispatched_date` and 15 `delivered_date` on orders whose `status` is `Delivered`, plus 5 `promised_date`. Do not confuse these with the legitimate blanks on In Transit / Awaiting Dispatch / Cancelled orders. |

### Operational problems

| Problem | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Backlog build-up** | During an autumn promotion (**12 to 25 October 2026**) demand rises 1.7× while capacity rises only about 1.2×. Open orders go from a normal ~30 to a peak of **266 on 25 October** and are not back under 40 until **7 November**. Late deliveries jump from 2–5% of orders in a normal week to 16% (orders placed week of 12 Oct), 72% (19 Oct) and 87% (26 Oct). |
| **Two chronically late suppliers** | **SUP-04 (Brenner Werkzeug GmbH, Germany)** and **SUP-09 (Shenlan Electronics Co Ltd, China)** deliver late about 63% and 65% of the time (3 to 12 days late) and short-deliver 41% and 30% of POs. Every other supplier is late 4–13% of the time and short-delivers 0–12%. |
| **12 SKUs that run out of stock** | These SKUs have reorder points set too low for their demand and lead time, so stock hits zero before replenishment arrives: `NB-10033`, `NB-10037`, `NB-10047`, `NB-10119`, `NB-10123`, `NB-10153`, `NB-10201`, `NB-10225`, `NB-10233`, `NB-10250`, `NB-10273`, `NB-10291`. Nine are supplied by SUP-04 or SUP-09. They are out of stock for between 19 and 50 of the 90 days. **No other SKU ever reaches zero.** |
| **Under-performing shift** | On peak days (12 to 25 October) the **Night shift** picks about 25% fewer units per hour (about 17.5 vs 22.9 normally). The Day shift is unaffected (about 23.5 vs 23.8). Outside the peak both shifts are steady. |

### Not planted, but worth knowing

- 18 orders are `Awaiting Dispatch` and 66 `In Transit` at the end of the period. These are normal end-of-window effects, not errors.
- 60 orders (about 1%) are `Cancelled` before dispatch.
- Carrier delays (a day or two late in about 7% of deliveries) are random background noise.
